# Research digest — Why mobile games fail (2016–2026) + retention benchmarks

Compiled 2026-07-31 for the PULL improvement report. Method: ~20 targeted searches; synthesized from search-result content of the primary sources listed (direct page fetches were partially blocked in the research sandbox).

## Failure patterns

1. **Fun core, no elder game — retention below bar kills even beloved games.** Supercell has killed 30+ titles with consistent stated reasons: enjoyable but doesn't hold people. Clash Mini ("a good game but not the game that would fulfill our dream"), Everdale (killed by Supercell, acquired by Metacore, relaunched, killed *again* — the retention problem was in the design, not the operator), Boom Beach: Frontlines (a year of redesigns never moved the needle), Rush Wars (repetitive), Hay Day Pop. *Lesson: moment-to-moment fun is table stakes; games die on week-2+ retention; design the return-reason in from the start.*
2. **"Solved acquisition, failed retention" — Squad Busters (2024), the definitive recent case.** Record Supercell launch, >$100M by end of 2024, still a failure: revenue −50% within months toward near-zero over 18 months; diagnosis: lackluster learning loop, muted character design, readability problems; "never soft-launched long enough to measure so much as a D30." A 2025 relaunch failed to reverse it. Paananen conceded the ~one-month soft launch tested nothing beyond D7. *Lesson: great first sessions and big installs prove nothing; averaging casual+midcore serves neither; content updates cannot fix an exhausting core.*
3. **Polish, awards, and IP don't save a game that doesn't fit its context or cost base.** Apex Legends Mobile: iPhone GOTY, 4.8 stars, killed May 2023 (oversaturated BR market, 300–500-person cost base). CoD: Warzone Mobile: ~50M pre-registrations, lost a reported 65% of players in three weeks, 2.4 Play Store rating (performance), gone by 2026. Warcraft Rumble: development ended 2025. *Lesson: quality ≠ product-market fit; console-fidelity thinking fails the mobile context; a tiny cost base is a strategic advantage.*
4. **Monetization that fights the player destroys trust even when it prints money.** Diablo Immortal: $300M+ year one and lasting brand damage. Mario Kart Tour: gacha backlash, lawsuit, gacha stripped 2022, shutters 2026. *Lesson: for an indie whose distribution is word-of-mouth, generosity is the only viable posture.*
5. **The hyper-casual collapse (2021–23): instant fun without depth stopped being a business.** ATT killed cheap UA; zero-retention design was the root exposure. Voodoo's pivot: hyper-casual fell to 25% of revenue; publishing bar moved from 55% D1 / 22% D7 / CPI<$0.25 to hybrid-casual thresholds of **45% D1, 15% D7 (20% puzzle), 10% D30** via light meta-progression bolted onto arcade cores ($0 → $250M hybrid revenue in 3 years). *Lesson: the game itself must retain; "instant fun + light meta" is the surviving template.*
6. **First-session confusion and slow time-to-fun kill D1.** Industry-average D1 ~27% — 73% never come back. Causes: long tutorials, no clear goal, permission prompts before trust, "high tutorial completion but low D1" (learning cost exceeded fun delivered). *Lesson: the first 60 seconds must be core gameplay; every screen before gameplay is D1 tax.*
7. **Difficulty spikes and unfair-feeling deaths are silent churn engines.** Players don't complain; they leave. Published churn case: level failure rates spiking 15%→75% across two levels correlating with mass drop-off ("the First Wall"). *Lesson: instrument where runs end; deaths the player can't attribute to their own mistake are the most expensive kind.*
8. **Tested well, failed live.** Novelty inflates first-session delight and short-cohort metrics; soft-launch orthodoxy: marginal soft-launch numbers get worse at scale, not better; only long cohorts are honest. (Brawl Stars soft-launched ~18 months; Squad Busters ~5 weeks.)
9. **Web-specific: losing players between sessions is the default.** No store presence, no icon, and on iOS effectively no push (Web Push only for home-screen apps since iOS 16.4, 2023). Discovery runs through portals (Poki ~60M MAU) or nothing. CrazyGames measured ~+18% retention when players installed as PWA. *Lesson: design re-engagement that needs no notification channel (habits, streaks, memorable URL, A2HS at the peak-joy moment); treat app-store distribution as the retention upgrade path.*

## Retention benchmarks

- GameAnalytics H1 2019 (classic bar): top games D1 40%, D7 15%, D28 6.5%; studios dropped prototypes with D1 < 35%.
- GameAnalytics 2025 (11,600 games, 2024 data): top-25% D1 only 26.5–27.7%; median D7 3.4–3.9%; 75% of all games have D28 < 3%. iOS top-quartile D1 (31–33%) beats Android (25–27%).
- Voodoo current hybrid-casual bar: 45% D1 / 15% D7 (20% puzzle) / 10% D30.
- Genre: match/puzzle lead D1 (~32%); **arcade shows the steepest D1→D7 drop of any genre (~−43%)**; median hyper-casual session ~6m42s.
- Rule-of-thumb DOA lines for a session-based arcade game: D1 < 30% = broken first session; D7 < 10% = no reason to return; D30 < 5% = no elder game.

## The arcade genre problem

Score-chasers: best genre at minute one, worst at week one. Score alone retains only the competitive minority; "beat my best" flattens when improvement slows. Monetization weak (ad-dependent; best-in-class hyper-casual ARPDAU ~$0.15). Premium is not an escape (paid fell to 1.8% of App Store revenue by 2019; Monument Valley saw only 5% paid installs on Android). Survivors changed the config: Crossy Road (collection meta + opt-in ads + cheap IAP, "putting players first"), Alto (rotating session goals over the endless core), hybrid-casual industrializing the same move. **Best-score-only meta is the configuration the last decade falsified.**
